Bioinformatics consulting

What you receive

  • Question and feasibility review
  • Method and tool selection with assumptions
  • Compute and reference-data requirements
  • Deliverable checklist and reproducibility plan
  • Written scope before work begins

How the work happens

  1. Review your question and inputs without collecting confidential files in the inquiry form.
  2. Agree methods, deliverables, compute, revisions and acceptance checks in writing.
  3. After feasibility is confirmed, run the agreed workflow with recorded versions and input provenance.
  4. Review outputs, explain limitations and hand over agreed materials.

Feasibility comes first.

Describe your assay, inputs and study design. Supported methods, tools and delivery capacity are confirmed during review; the items below are possible scope elements, not a commitment to accept every dataset.

Scope before a price.

A focused review, complete analytical workflow and custom computational project have different requirements. Sample count, assay, reference quality, compute, urgency and interpretation affect the scope. Request an itemized quote; prices and timelines are agreed after assessment.

Analysis cannot guarantee publication, statistical significance or biological validation.

Common questions

Can I inquire before my data are ready?

Yes. Choose your current stage and describe the study question. Feasibility and planning can be scoped separately.

Do I need to upload my dataset?

No. Do not send confidential or participant data through the inquiry form. Any transfer is agreed through an appropriate secure route.

What affects cost and turnaround?

Data quality and scale, study design, reference resources, compute, deliverables and revisions. We confirm an agreed scope rather than promising a fixed deadline from a short form.

What computational decision needs to be made next?

Bring these inputs to scoping

  • A focused biological question and the decision you need the analysis to inform.
  • An inventory of available files, previous methods, software versions and existing reports.
  • Known constraints on data access, compute, budget, timing and team responsibilities.

Prerequisites and interpretation limits

A feasibility review may recommend a narrower question or further data collection. A written method plan is distinct from implementing and validating a production workflow.
